What this suggests

  • Top value adders (metals, vehicles, chemicals, pharma) are heavier on capital and skills. This is consistent with Kochhar et al. (2006).
  • Food, textiles and apparel absorb many workers at low value per worker, consistent with a labour-intensive, low-productivity base.
  • Coke & Petroleum's output per worker is inflated by crude oil costs. It stays first on value added per worker, but the gap narrows sharply.
  • Pharmaceuticals ranks 2nd on value added per worker, consistent with a knowledge-intensive industry. This study has no R&D or skills data to test that.

Method and limits

Method. ASI [2023-2024] industry totals in ₹ lakh. Productivity is output or net value added divided by workers. Built in Python (pandas, matplotlib).

  • ASI covers registered factories only, so informal manufacturing is excluded (Kathuria, Raj and Sen 2010).
  • One year of industry totals shows association, not cause. Testing misallocation needs plant-level data (Hsieh and Klenow 2009).
  • Small industries such as Publishing (about 11,000 workers) can look extreme per worker.
  • Next steps: capital per worker, wage share, several ASI years.

Source: Annual Survey of Industries, MoSPI, Government of India. Ratios and shares are my calculations from industry totals.
